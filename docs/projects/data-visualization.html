About the dataset

Our project uses graphics processing unit (GPU) information from years beginning in 2009. The analyses use G2DMark graphics quality scores, thermal design power (TDP), power-performance scores, manufacturer names, OpenCL benchmarks, GPU prices, and calendar years. Using Microsoft Excel, we organized these measurements to answer four different questions through a histogram, scatter plot, bar chart, and time-series visualization.

The original Excel workbook and dataset source URL were not supplied with the presentation, so the exact source and cleaning operations still need to be confirmed.

Visualization 1 of 4

G2DMark scores show a right-skewed distribution

Our question: How are GPUs distributed across G2DMark (2D graphics quality) scores?

Why this interested us: We wanted to see whether 2D graphics scores were spread evenly or whether most GPUs clustered at lower values.

How we used Excel: In Excel, scores were grouped into intervals and the number of GPUs in each interval was counted. The team’s histogram shows larger counts in lower ranges and a long tail of high scores.

How we developed the slide: We used a histogram with score intervals along the x-axis, frequency on the y-axis, and an assertion-style title stating the right-skewed result.

Visualization 2 of 4

Thermal Design Power has a weak relationship with power performance

Our question: Is GPU thermal design power (TDP) strongly associated with power-performance score?

Why this interested us: Power requirements affect hardware choices and energy considerations. We wanted to find out how much a GPU’s TDP tells us about its score.

How we used Excel: Excel was used to plot TDP in watts against power-performance score for individual GPUs. A linear trendline was added; its R² value is 0.0403, so it explains about 4% of the variation in the score.

How we developed the slide: We displayed each GPU as a point, labeled both axes, retained the dotted trendline and R² value, and placed the conclusion in the headline.

Visualization 3 of 4

Apple and Nvidia have the highest average OpenCL scores in our dataset

Our question: How do average OpenCL benchmark scores compare by GPU manufacturer?

Why this interested us: We wanted to compare manufacturers in a way that highlighted performance differences among the GPUs represented in our data.

How we used Excel: Excel was used to group GPUs by manufacturer and calculate average OpenCL scores. The horizontal bar chart compares AMD, Apple, ARM, Intel, Nvidia, Other, Qualcomm, and Samsung.

How we developed the slide: We used horizontal bars so all manufacturers could be read easily and an assertion headline to call attention to Apple and Nvidia as the highest averages in this dataset.

Visualization 4 of 4

Average GPU prices rose overall between 2009 and 2022

Our question: How did average GPU prices change from 2009 to 2022?

Why this interested us: Prices help explain the cost of GPU hardware, and we wanted to see how the average changed over calendar years.

How we used Excel: We used Excel to summarize GPU prices by year, then created a time-series line graph with year on the horizontal axis and average price in U.S. dollars on the vertical axis.

How we developed the slide: We kept the graph as a chronological line chart and added an assertion headline describing the overall increase. The original slide attributes the increase to demand, although the chart alone cannot prove that cause.

Project takeaways

Our four charts show that the G2DMark distribution is right-skewed, TDP has little linear relationship with the plotted power-performance score, Apple and Nvidia have the highest average OpenCL scores in this dataset, and average GPU prices generally rose over the years shown. Each original slide pairs a clear assertion with visual evidence.
